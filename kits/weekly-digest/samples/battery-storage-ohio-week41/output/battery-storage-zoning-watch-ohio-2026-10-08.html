Top item  Tarrow County · Oct 6, 2026

Tarrow County to consider a 12-month pause on battery storage zoning applications

Commissioners will take up Resolution 2026-118 at their October 13 regular session. It would stop the county from accepting or processing zoning permits, rezonings (zoning map amendments) and conditional use requests for battery energy storage facilities of 1 MW or more for 12 months.

It covers the areas under county zoning, currently Ferris, Quill and Brindle townships; townships that run their own zoning are not affected. Applications filed and found complete before it takes effect, and batteries that serve a single home or business on the same property, are excluded.

Why it matters: As drafted, the pause takes effect on the day it is adopted. The county zoning commission would then have nine months to recommend setback, fire-safety and decommissioning standards.

Zoning rules · 1

Wrenfield Township, Tarrow County · Oct 5, 2026

Wrenfield Township proposes battery storage rules: I-1 district only, 300-foot setbacks, removal bond

Zoning text amendment ZTA-2026-03 would add definitions for battery energy storage systems and allow battery energy storage facilities only as a conditional use in the I-1 Light Industrial district. It would require setbacks of at least 300 feet from existing homes and 100 feet from property lines, a decommissioning plan backed by a bond or letter of credit and reviewed every five years, and a fire-safety plan reviewed by the fire chief before a zoning certificate is issued.

Why it matters: Written comments close on October 16. After the October 21 hearing, the township zoning commission sends its recommendation to the township trustees.

Ordinances · 1

City of Marlowe Falls, Halden County · Oct 5, 2026

Marlowe Falls battery fire-safety ordinance amended ahead of its second reading

The Safety Committee amended Ordinance 2026-41 on October 5. As amended, it applies to battery energy storage systems larger than 600 kWh (up from 50 kWh), other than systems serving a single one- or two-family home. It requires compliance with NFPA 855, an emergency response plan approved by the fire chief before a certificate of occupancy is issued, a site walkthrough with the fire department once a year (down from twice a year) and a 24-hour emergency contact posted at each entrance.

Why it matters: Systems of 600 kWh or less would not be covered. City council holds the second reading on October 19.

City council second reading: Mon, Oct 19, 2026, 7:00 PM ET
