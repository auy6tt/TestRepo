Ohio · Week 41 · Thursday, October 8, 2026 · Issue 12

THIS WEEK

Two local governments in our coverage area moved toward new battery
storage rules this week, and two projects reached public review.

The item to watch is Tarrow County's proposed 12-month pause on battery
storage zoning applications. Commissioners are scheduled to consider it
on Tuesday, October 13, and as drafted it would take effect as soon as
it is adopted.

MORATORIA
---------

[TOP ITEM] Tarrow County to consider a 12-month pause on battery storage
zoning applications
  Tarrow County · Oct 6, 2026
  Commissioners will take up Resolution 2026-118 at their October 13
  regular session. It would stop the county from accepting or processing
  zoning permits, rezonings (zoning map amendments) and conditional use
  requests for battery energy storage facilities of 1 MW or more for 12
  months.
  It covers the areas under county zoning, currently Ferris, Quill and
  Brindle townships; townships that run their own zoning are not
  affected. Applications filed and found complete before it takes
  effect, and batteries that serve a single home or business on the same
  property, are excluded.
  Why it matters: As drafted, the pause takes effect on the day it is
  adopted. The county zoning commission would then have nine months to
  recommend setback, fire-safety and decommissioning standards.
  Resolution: 2026-118
  Covers: Battery storage facilities of 1 MW or more
  Length: 12 months from adoption, unless ended earlier
  Commissioners' session (public comment at the start): Tue, Oct 13,
  2026, 9:30 AM ET, Commissioners' Chambers, Tarrow County
  Administration Building, 41 Court Street, Tarrow City
  Source: Tarrow County Board of Commissioners, agenda for October 13,
  2026 (PDF), page 2, item 7

ZONING RULES
------------

Wrenfield Township proposes battery storage rules: I-1 district only,
300-foot setbacks, removal bond
  Wrenfield Township, Tarrow County · Oct 5, 2026
  Zoning text amendment ZTA-2026-03 would add definitions for battery
  energy storage systems and allow battery energy storage facilities
  only as a conditional use in the I-1 Light Industrial district. It
  would require setbacks of at least 300 feet from existing homes and
  100 feet from property lines, a decommissioning plan backed by a bond
  or letter of credit and reviewed every five years, and a fire-safety
  plan reviewed by the fire chief before a zoning certificate is issued.
  Why it matters: Written comments close on October 16. After the
  October 21 hearing, the township zoning commission sends its
  recommendation to the township trustees.
  Amendment: ZTA-2026-03
  Setbacks: 300 ft from existing homes, 100 ft from property lines
  Where allowed: I-1 Light Industrial, as a conditional use
  Written comments due to the Zoning Inspector: Fri, Oct 16, 2026, 4:00
  PM ET
  Township zoning commission public hearing: Wed, Oct 21, 2026, 7:00 PM
  ET, Wrenfield Township Hall, 8 Gable Road
  Source: Wrenfield Township, notice of public hearing on ZTA-2026-03
  (PDF), proposed amendments 1 to 5
  https://www.wrenfieldtwp.example/notices/ZTA-2026-03-hearing-notice.pdf

ORDINANCES
----------

Marlowe Falls battery fire-safety ordinance amended ahead of its second
reading
  City of Marlowe Falls, Halden County · Oct 5, 2026
  The Safety Committee amended Ordinance 2026-41 on October 5. As
  amended, it applies to battery energy storage systems larger than 600
  kWh (up from 50 kWh), other than systems serving a single one- or two-
  family home. It requires compliance with NFPA 855, an emergency
  response plan approved by the fire chief before a certificate of
  occupancy is issued, a site walkthrough with the fire department once
  a year (down from twice a year) and a 24-hour emergency contact posted
  at each entrance.
  Why it matters: Systems of 600 kWh or less would not be covered. City
  council holds the second reading on October 19.
  City council second reading: Mon, Oct 19, 2026, 7:00 PM ET, Council
  Chambers, Marlowe Falls City Hall, 12 Main Street
  Source: City of Marlowe Falls, Ordinance 2026-41 as amended (PDF),
  sections 1 to 5; committee amendments on page 2
