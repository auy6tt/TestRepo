# Written Hazard Communication Programme: [Company name]

> **DRAFT FOR THE EMPLOYER TO ADAPT.** This outline follows the general structure of the sample written programme that OSHA publishes for small businesses, rewritten in plain words. It is not legal advice and it is not a finished programme. The employer completes every section, adapts it to the site, adopts it and keeps it up to date.
>
> Check the current text of OSHA's Hazard Communication Standard (29 CFR 1910.1200) and its compliance dates on osha.gov. OSHA updated the standard in 2024; the employer compliance date for single-substance chemicals is reported as 20 November 2026, with later dates for mixtures. Small businesses can get free, confidential help from OSHA's On-Site Consultation Program.
>

## 1. Purpose and scope

- [Company name] uses hazardous chemicals at [site address(es)]. This programme explains how we tell employees about those chemicals: through labels, safety data sheets and training.
- It applies to [all employees who may be exposed to hazardous chemicals during normal work or in a foreseeable emergency].
- Programme coordinator: [name, job title, phone, email].

## 2. Who does what

- **Programme coordinator:** keeps this programme, the chemical list and the safety data sheets up to date, and arranges training.
- **Supervisors:** [make sure employees in their area know where the sheets are, that containers are labelled, and that new staff are trained before they work with chemicals].
- **Employees:** [follow labels and training, keep labels in place, report missing or damaged labels and missing sheets].
- **Purchasing:** [whoever orders chemicals checks that a safety data sheet comes with every new product and passes it to the coordinator].

## 3. List of hazardous chemicals

- The list is kept [at the front of the SDS binder and in the file inventory.xlsx, "Chemical List" sheet; see Appendix A].
- Each product is listed by the same name (product identifier) that appears on its label and its safety data sheet.
- The list is updated by [name] when [a new product arrives, a product is no longer used, and at least every (period)].

## 4. Safety data sheets

- The coordinator keeps a current safety data sheet for every hazardous chemical on the list.
- Sheets are kept [location of each binder, for example: service office, paint room door] and [electronic copy: where, and on which device].
- Employees must be able to reach the sheets in their work area during every shift. [Describe how, and what happens during a power or network outage if you rely on electronic copies.]
- New or updated sheets: [who checks each delivery for a new or updated sheet and replaces the old one].
- Missing sheets: [who asks the manufacturer, importer or distributor for the sheet, and how soon].
- Replaced sheets: [your policy for keeping old sheets. Ask your safety adviser what records you must keep].

## 5. Labels and other forms of warning

- Containers received from suppliers keep their original labels. Labels must not be removed or defaced, and must stay readable.
- Containers we fill ourselves (spray bottles, transfer cans): [describe the labelling method you use].
- [Name] checks labels [how often]. Employees who find a missing or damaged label [tell their supervisor; the container is not used until it is labelled].
- Small containers filled for immediate use by one person: [describe your rule, and check the conditions in the standard].

## 6. Employee information and training

- Training is given by [a qualified person: name or organisation].
- Employees are trained when they are first assigned to work with hazardous chemicals, and again whenever a new chemical hazard is brought into their work area.
- Training covers (the trainer fills in the detail):
  - the requirements of the standard and this programme, and where the programme, the chemical list and the safety data sheets are kept
  - the operations in their work area where hazardous chemicals are present
  - how to tell when a hazardous chemical is present or has been released
  - the physical and health hazards of the chemicals in their work area
  - how employees protect themselves: work practices, emergency procedures and protective equipment
  - how to read labels and safety data sheets, and how to find and use the information
- Training records: [optional: how you record who was trained, by whom and when].

## 7. Non-routine tasks

- Non-routine tasks that may involve hazardous chemicals: [for example: cleaning tanks or pits, unblocking drains, repairing equipment that held chemicals].
- Before such a task, [the supervisor] tells the employees about the hazards and the protective measures. [Describe how.]

## 8. Chemicals in unlabelled pipes

- [Delete if not relevant. Otherwise describe how employees are told what is in the pipes before they work on them.]

## 9. Contractors and other employers on site

- When contractors work at the site, [name] tells them where our safety data sheets are kept, explains our labelling method, and tells them about chemicals they may be exposed to.
- Contractors who bring hazardous chemicals on site give us [their safety data sheets and labelling information] before work starts.

## 10. Where this programme is kept, and reviews

- This written programme is kept [location] and is available to employees and their representatives on request, and to OSHA.
- Reviewed by [name] on [date]. Next review: [date or event, for example when products change].
